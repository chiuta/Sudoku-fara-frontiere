#### 132. Latin cube 3D · Latin cube 3D (`cube3`)

**Every line of the cube holds each digit once.**

The N×N×N cube is unfolded into N layers placed side by side (separated by a gap). Every line in each of the three directions — row in a layer, column in a layer, and “in depth” (the same position in all layers) — holds each digit once. Sizes 4, 5, 6. The cube is very rigid: at “Hard” few givens remain. The view is layered; you can switch to “3D view” (drag to rotate the cube, the slider hides the front layers; click to select cells).

#### 133. Cube with 2×2 boxes · Cube with 2×2 boxes (`cubebox`)

**4×4×4 Latin cube with 2×2 boxes in every layer.**

As in the Latin cube 3D (4×4×4), but each layer additionally has four 2×2 boxes (thick outlines) holding each digit once: every layer is a 4×4 sudoku, and the “depth” lines also hold each digit once. 4×4×4 only.

#### 134. Hypercube 4D · Hypercube 4D (`hyper4`)

**Sudoku in four dimensions.**

The N×N×N×N hypercube is unfolded as an N×N mosaic of N×N layers (the first two coordinates pick the layer: layer row and layer column). Every line along any of the four axes holds each digit once. Sizes 3 (81 cells) and 4 (256 cells). Very rigid: few givens.

#### 136. Greco-Latin (orthogonal squares) · Greco-Latin (orthogonal squares) (`greco`)

**Two overlaid Latin squares: every digit–letter pair appears once.**

Two N×N squares side by side: the left one with digits, the right one with letters (A, B, C…; when filling in, the key “1/A” means digit 1 on the left or letter A on the right). In each square every row and column holds each symbol once. In addition the two squares are orthogonal: looking at the same position in both squares, the pair (digit, letter) is different in all N² positions — every possible pair appears exactly once. Sizes 3, 4, 5, 7 (for 6 no such pair exists, 8 and 9 are left out for speed). At 7×7 “Hard” generation can take about 10–15 s.
