<a id="antibishop"></a>
#### 102. Anti-alfil (`antibishop`)

**No equal digits two diagonal steps apart.**

In addition to the classic rules, two cells exactly two steps apart diagonally (2 rows and 2 columns away; the fairy-chess “alfil” leap) cannot hold the same digit. This is the 2×2 leap, not the sliding bishop (which would force whole diagonals). Sizes 9×9 and 6×6.

<a id="torusking"></a>
#### 105. Torus anti-king (`torusking`)

**Anti-king on a grid that wraps like a torus.**

The grid continues across the edges: the top edge is glued to the bottom one, left to right. Two cells a king’s move apart (diagonal neighbours), including across an edge, cannot hold the same digit (e.g. the top-left corner is a diagonal neighbour of the bottom-right one). 9×9 only.

#### 108. Möbius anti-king (`mobiusking`)

**Left glued to right with a twist.**

#### 109. Klein anti-king (`kleinking`)

**Klein bottle: left–right twisted, top–bottom plain.**

The left edge is glued to the right edge with a twist (row r joins row 10−r), and top is glued to bottom without a twist: a Klein bottle. Two cells a king’s move apart, including across seams, cannot hold the same digit. 9×9 only. The gluing convention is the one described here.

<a id="projking"></a>
#### 110. Projective-plane anti-king (`projking`)

**Both pairs of edges glued with a twist.**

Left is glued to right with a twist (row r joins 10−r) and top to bottom with a twist (column c joins 10−c): a real projective plane. Two cells a king’s move apart, including across seams, cannot hold the same digit. 9×9 only. The gluing convention is the one described here.
